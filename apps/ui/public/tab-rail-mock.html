The fore edge, without the rotation

The rail is right where it belongs — a tabbed manual puts its divisions on the fore edge, and a sidebar here would make this a dashboard wearing a manual's colours. The defect is narrower than that: the labels are laid on their side in a 52 pixel column, and a rotated word is read appreciably slower than an upright one. On the one control whose entire job is answering which section am I in, that is the wrong place to spend a reader's attention.

Worth saying plainly, because it decides the whole question: a real tabbed divider does not rotate its lettering either. Rotation belongs to the thumb index cut into a dictionary's fore edge, not to the tab card standing out of a binder. Every option here is more faithful to the form than what ships today, not a concession made to legibility.

All four frames below are driven by one set of controls, so they can be compared on the same state. Nothing in the app has been changed yet; this is for review.

Option A · The printed divider

This is the one to ship. The rail widens to 9.25 rem, the width a divider tab is actually printed at, and the lettering stands up. Everything load-bearing survives untouched: navigation stays on the fore edge, tab height stays proportional to each division's extent so the rail is still a picture of the book, selection is still the punched hole, locked divisions are still face-down rather than hidden. The punch moves to the tab's leading edge, where a binder is actually punched, and the label sets from a constant left margin, so the open section reads as a card pulled further out of the book, which is the one thing the extension was always trying to say.

Cost 6 rem of page width. The leaf is capped at 78 rem, so on anything from a laptop up it comes out of margin rather than out of content.

Option B · The narrow fore edge

The smallest change that fixes the defect. The rail grows by 20 pixels, to 4.5 rem, and the label is set upright in Archivo at width 62, which is exactly enough for all four current labels to sit on one line, including RAW LAKE. Upright and small beats sideways and large: the eye reads word shapes, and a rotated word does not have one.

Cost No headroom. CUSTOMERS measures 53 px in a 55 px column: it fits and nothing more. A longer division name wraps to two lines or has to be renamed, so the wheel's three spare hues come with a naming constraint attached that nothing in the code will remind anyone about.

Option C · Top tabs

The other real divider form: the exhibit-binder index, tabs across the head. Extent drives width instead of height, so the rail is still the picture. The argument for it is not legibility. It is that the phone breakpoint already renders this, at the foot of the leaf. Adopt it on desktop and the app stops maintaining two navigations that behave differently and get tested separately.

Cost The fore edge goes, and with it the strongest thing the interface says about what it is. This is the option to pick only if the width in A is genuinely unaffordable.

What every option holds

Invariant How it survives
Extent Still flex-grow from DIVISIONS[].extent — height in A and B, width in C. Never measured from the current customer's rows, so the navigation cannot move under the cursor.
Selection Still the punched hole, never a colour change and never a bar — so aria-current has a visible partner that survives greyscale.
Face-down A locked division still renders turned over, with the reason in its accessible name. Nothing vanishes, so nobody has to already know the People tab exists.
Lettering Still chosen per hue by letteringOn: ink on chrome yellow at 8.75:1, paper on oxide 4.98, teal 5.05, ultramarine 7.50. A hue added later is lettered correctly without anyone remembering to think about it.
Face-down ink Darkened from #5f5844 to #524a37, 4.37:1 → 5.42:1 on the hatch. A disabled control is WCAG-exempt, but this one carries the sentence explaining why a section will not open, and the stylesheet's own note says an unreadable label is a defect whatever the exemption says.
Motion Still the two-frame hinge on a transform, still nothing animated that would reflow the rail, still nothing at all under prefers-reduced-motion.
